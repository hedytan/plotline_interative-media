# The Landlord's Walk: concept

**Live:** https://hedytan.github.io/plotline_interative-media/walk/

## What it is
An interactive artwork. It takes Monopoly's rules and turns them into a lived experience on a street that generates itself as you walk.

## Who it is for
Young people who rent, and anyone who has played Monopoly and "won".

## What it says
**Your win is someone else's loss.** In Round 1 you are the landlord and you win. In Round 2 you walk the same street as the family you pushed out.

This is the warning Lizzie Magie built into *The Landlord's Game* in 1904, before Parker Brothers sold it as Monopoly in 1935.

## How it plays (about a minute each round)
1. **Round 1, you are the landlord.** One street. Press E at a FOR SALE home to buy it. Buy 3 homes. There is no money to manage.
2. **Round 2, you are them.** You are the first family you evicted, carrying a moving box. You walk past the homes you bought ("OWNED BY YOU"). Every home for rent is too dear. At each corner you are pushed into a poorer town further out, until you can rent a caravan in The Fringe, 1h 35m from work.
3. **End card:** "Your win was their loss", then Magie's story.

## How each interaction carries the meaning

| Interaction | What the player sees or hears | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | The camera starts above a board-game board and dives into one square, which becomes the street | You are entering the game, as a lived place |
| Walking (↑) | Homes rise out of the ground ahead of you | The landscape is generated as you move |
| Stopping beside a home for sale | The camera turns to face it; the family out the front speaks ("The kids grew up here.") | A listing is someone's home |
| Buying (E) | A SOLD stamp and the family's name; they carry boxes away; the music stops for a moment | Your win is their loss |
| Buying a second or third home | The real house sinks; a giant plastic Monopoly house (then a red hotel) drops onto the lot | To the winner, a home is only a game piece |
| Buying more | The street loses colour, the music goes muffled, neighbours fold their arms ("Landlord.") | Displacement and isolation build up |
| Winning | Every house ahead becomes a grey plastic piece | The winner's street is a board, not a place |
| Round 2: the box | You carry a moving box in front of you the whole time | You are now the one who had to leave |
| Round 2: your old street | Your homes carry "OWNED BY YOU" signs | The same street, from the other side |
| Round 2: your old home | The family's things on the kerb: a mattress, a kid's bike, a family photo, a box of toys, a teddy, a sign saying FREE | What a sale leaves behind |
| Round 2: applying (E) | "TOO DEAR · $470 a week. You can pay $230. 50 other people applied too." | Unaffordable rent and competition |
| The city on the horizon | The further out the town, the smaller and further away the city skyline at the end of the street | Urban sprawl: the city you work in moves out of reach |
| Investor towers | "THE PINNACLE · 300 LUXURY APARTMENTS" with a red neon "0 AFFORDABLE · MOSTLY EMPTY" | Apartments built by the rich, not affordable housing |
| Round 1: corners (← / →) | You choose: a poorer town or a richer one, and buy wherever you like | The winner has every choice |
| Round 2: corners | You are turned, without choosing, into poorer towns; signposts give the commute | Urban sprawl: pushed further from work |
| A harsher street | Empty investor towers wear a giant top hat and a red neon "0 AFFORDABLE · MOSTLY EMPTY"; the sky is smog | Wealth built for investors, not for people |
| Slogans painted on the walls | One wall a block carries a slogan painted by hand in red: "300 HOMES. 0 YOU CAN AFFORD.", "LUXURY APARTMENTS. AFFORDABLE TO NOBODY.", "CAN'T AFFORD THE CITY? MOVE TO THE EDGE." | The street itself speaks the artwork's message, with nothing else in the way |
| Endings, from above | The camera rises out of the street: in Round 1 the whole city is a grey board with SOLD on every square and only your pieces in colour (POPULATION: YOU); in Round 2 a dashed red line runs from the suburbs to The Fringe, where your box is | The city as the game sees it |
| The sound of homes | Every lived-in home has its own sound through the wall: kids, a dog, a piano, the TV. Stand still and it comes through clearly. Buy it and it goes silent | A home is somebody's life; buying it takes that life off the street |
| Round 2: sound | Plates rattle in your box with each step; your old home echoes once, faintly; each rejection is a phone notification | Memory and loss, heard rather than read |
| Music | One clear style per class of town: strings and a harp for the rich, a house beat in the city, pop in the suburbs, a slow broken piano with no beat out on the poor edge | You can hear who a street is for |

## What is generated, and how
- **The street.** Each town is a loop of ten 48 m blocks. A block's buildings, lot sizes, colours, trees, billboards, sandwich boards, tents and slogans come from a random generator seeded by the town and the block's place in the loop, so the same town looks the same each time you come back, and every town is different.
- **Six towns, one per class.** Each has its own building types (castles, villas, mansions, towers, bungalows, shacks, caravans), prices, rents, skyline and music.
- **Growth as you walk.** Lots are outlined plots until you approach; then the buildings rise out of the ground. Time is distance walked: every 120 m is a year, and some homes are redeveloped into empty investor towers. Investors buy some homes for sale just before you reach them.
- **Your actions change it.** Bought homes turn into plastic pieces; the street loses colour and sound as you buy; billboards switch to flattering the landlord; Round 2 is generated from the same seed, keeping what you did.
- **Sound** is synthesised live (Web Audio): each home's sound, the band, footsteps and effects.

## Research and influences
- Lizzie Magie, *The Landlord's Game* (patented 1904), and its two rule sets (monopolist and anti-monopolist, "Prosperity").
- Henry George's land value tax, which her second rule set is based on.
- The history of Monopoly: Charles Darrow sold the game to Parker Brothers in 1935; Magie was paid $500.
- Australian housing: deposits, negative gearing, rent rises, the commute from outer suburbs.

## Changes made after tutor feedback
- **Play-test:** the meaning still did not come through: there was too much to manage (money, loans, pay day, two rule sets, choosing towns). **Change:** cut to one idea in two rounds: you win as the landlord, then walk the same street as the family you evicted.
- **Feedback:** the meaning was unclear: a Monopoly game, or an artwork? **Change:** it is framed as an artwork from the opening card onwards, and this page states who it is for and what it says.
- **Feedback:** interactions were not all meaningful. **Change:** each interaction is listed above with what it means; features that do not carry the meaning were cut (shift work, casinos, jail).
- **Feedback:** suggested satirical signs and commentary on sprawl and unaffordable apartments. **Change:** billboards now react to the player; investor towers sit empty; signposts show the commute.
- **Feedback:** planning had no timeline. **Change:** the timeline above.
